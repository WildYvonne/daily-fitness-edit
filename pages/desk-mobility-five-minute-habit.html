Five-Minute Desk Mobility I Do Between Zoom Calls

By Casey Nguyen
Share:
Published on Apr 22, 2026

I do not roll out a mat at the office. I set a timer for five minutes between calls: neck resets, thoracic opens against the chair, and hip shifts that remind my body it still bends.

The goal is not flexibility for Instagram. It is leaving the desk feeling less welded. On heavy meeting days, this tiny block is the difference between a headache and a normal evening.

Why five minutes is enough

Frequency beats duration. A short loop you will actually repeat beats a twenty-minute routine you skip because it feels dramatic.

I set a phone alarm labeled “reset” instead of relying on motivation. When it fires, I stand, roll shoulders back, and do ten slow breaths with ribs expanding sideways. That alone cuts the “I have been frozen in a chair for three hours” feeling more than I want to admit.

The loop I repeat (no equipment)

Chin tucks and gentle neck rotations first — nothing aggressive. Then thoracic extension over the chair back: hands interlaced behind the head, elbows wide, small arch through the upper back. Hip shifts and seated figure-four if space allows, or standing hip hinges holding the desk edge for balance.

Wrists get love too: prayer stretch, reverse prayer, ten slow fists opening to spread fingers. Typing injuries are fitness injuries if your job is mostly keys.

What I stopped doing

I stopped waiting for a “real” break. If I have sixty seconds between two documents, I take one deep squat hold at my desk and roll my ankles. It looks odd once; then nobody notices because everyone is on video half-dressed anyway.

The goal is not to become a mobility influencer. It is to end the workday with a neck that does not hate you when you try to check blind spots in the car. Small stakes, real payoff.
